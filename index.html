Concept of Operations (ConOps): General-Purpose Mobile Robot Platform for MARS Research

Download PDF (v1.0)

Establishes the operational context, mission objectives (O-01 to O-09), user classes, operational configurations, 11 operational modes, 7 representative scenarios (Scenario A – Scenario G), safety/maintenance concepts, and 16 open engineering items (OI-01 – OI-16) for the general-purpose MARS mobile robot platform.

SEC 1–3 Mission, Objectives & Stakeholders

Defines 9 operational objectives (O-01 Research Flexibility to O-09 Operational Reliability) across Principal Investigators, Researchers, Developers, Operators, Students, and Technicians.

Standard: ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018

SEC 4–5 System Overview & 5 Configurations

Supports (A) Independent, (B) Cooperative Multi-Robot, (C) Composite (physically docked), (D) Reconfigurable, and (E) Mixed operational configurations across indoor/outdoor and constrained comms environments.

Configs: Independent · Cooperative · Composite · Reconfigurable · Mixed

SEC 6–7 Applications, 8 Workflow Phases & 11 Modes

Covers Self-Reconfigurable Systems, Cooperative Transport, Navigation, SAR, and Region Sweeping across 8 experimental phases and 11 operational modes (including Manual Wireless Control).

Control: Manual · Centralized · Decentralized · Hybrid

SEC 8–15 7 Scenarios (A–G), Safety & 16 Open Issues

Details step-by-step operational sequences for Scenarios A–G, Human-System Interaction, Safety & Risk Considerations, Maintenance/Recovery, and 16 Open Issues (OI-01 to OI-16).

Traceability: Links StRS, OpsCon, PRD & SyRS

System Operational Concept (OpsCon): General-Purpose Research-Class Lightweight Mobile Robot Platform for MARS

Translates MARS-OPS-001 into a system-specific operational concept for a Research-Class Lightweight Mobile Robot (RCLMR, <1 kg) platform across 17 sections: system boundary, 6 functional elements, 4-dimensional configuration model, 11 operational modes, user authority hierarchy, 9-phase experiment execution, composite robot operations, ISO 12100 inherent low-risk safety, and 15 open issues (OI-01 – OI-15).

SEC 1–3 System Boundary, 6 Elements & RCLMR (<1 kg)

Defines internal/external system boundaries, 6 functional elements, and the RCLMR (<1 kg) lightweight classification enabling desktop-to-laboratory multi-robot deployment.

Related Doc: MARS-OPS-001 · Standard: ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018

SEC 4–6 4D Configuration Model & 11 Operational Modes

Specifies Physical (Single/Multi/Composite/Reconfigurable), Hardware, Control, and Network configuration dimensions alongside 11 operational modes and mode-transition triggers.

Modes: Off · Init · Idle · Manual · Auto · Coop · Composite · Fault

SEC 7–10 Authority Hierarchy, Comms & 9-Phase Execution

Establishes the Control Authority Hierarchy (Safety Stop > Operator Override > Manual Control > Autonomous/Coordinator), 4 comms patterns, 9 experiment phases, and physical docking/undocking.

Workflow: Setup · Init · Verify · Calibrate · Execute · Log · Recover

SEC 11–17 Abnormal Recovery, ISO 12100 Safety & 15 Open Issues

Details abnormal event handling, ISO 12100 inherent low-kinetic-energy safety philosophy, battery/maintenance lifecycle, constraints, and 15 open engineering items (OI-01 to OI-15).

Safety: Inherently Safe RCLMR Design (ISO 12100)
MARS-REQ-002 Version v1.0 (5 Pages) Baseline Release Author: Dhamdhawach Horsuwan

Concept of Operations (CONOPS): Requirements for a Mobile Robot Platform in MARS Research

Download PDF (v1.0)

Addresses the operational complexity of deploying and maintaining multiple robots concurrently, prioritizing reliability, modularity, ease of maintenance, cost-effectiveness, commercial-grade usability, and centralized fleet monitoring paired with decentralized agent autonomy.

CONOPS-1 1. Modularity and Extensibility

Standardized mechanical, electrical, and communication interfaces enabling hardware/software reuse and rapid sensor or actuator integration without redesigning the robot.

Use Case: Research Adaptability

CONOPS-2 2. Power Management (2.1 – 2.5)

Dedicated motor/logic voltage regulation, integrated BMS safety protection, hot-swappable power modules, centralized multi-port/dock fleet charging, and accessible rated power/emergency switches.

Use Cases: Motor Consistency · BMS Safety · Rapid Swap · Fleet Charging

CONOPS-3 3. Mobility and Manipulation (3.1 – 3.2)

Omnidirectional/holonomic drive with closed-loop encoder feedback for trajectory consistency, plus modular mechanical attachments (claws, electromagnets, vacuum end-effectors).

Use Cases: Formation Control · Collaborative Object Transport

CONOPS-4 4. Perception and Sensing (4.1)

High-frequency, low-latency onboard spatial perception for local obstacle avoidance and peer-agent detection in high-density multi-robot deployments.

Use Cases: Obstacle Avoidance · Reciprocal Collision Avoidance

CONOPS-5 5. Localization and State Estimation (5.1)

Robust pose estimation fusing onboard wheel odometry and IMU data with external tracking or vision-based localization within a shared coordinate frame.

Use Case: Global Coordinate Alignment

CONOPS-6 6. Multi-Agent Coordination & Comms (6.1 – 6.2)

Low-latency peer-to-peer inter-robot communication and dynamic decentralized network topologies capable of operating without fixed wireless infrastructure.

Use Cases: Distributed Task Allocation · Infrastructure-Free Comms

CONOPS-7 7. User Interface and Fleet Management (7.1 – 7.4)

Centralized fleet telemetry/command dashboard decoupled from agent autonomy, onboard graphical display with mode/calibration button, multi-color status LEDs, and fleet-wide experimental data logging software.

Use Cases: Experiment Management · Onboard Diagnostics · Visual Fleet Diagnostics · Performance Evaluation

Stakeholder Needs and Requirements

Download PDF (v1.1)

Identifies the six intended stakeholder groups (SH-01 MARS Researchers, SH-02 Robotics & Hardware Developers, SH-03 Software & Algorithm Developers, SH-04 Experimental Operators, SH-05 Laboratory Technicians, SH-06 External Research Collaborators), defines a three-tier priority scheme (Essential, Important, Desirable), and distinguishes researcher-perspective Research Needs (RN-01 – RN-08) from derived platform Stakeholder Requirements (SR-01 – SR-08):

RN-01 / SR-01 1. Scalability of Multi-Agent Experiments

Need (RN-01): Researchers need to conduct experiments involving different numbers of autonomous robots to investigate how fleet size affects coordination, communication, and task allocation.
Req (SR-01): Scalable, cost-effective unit design supporting varying fleet sizes.

Priority: Essential · Stakeholders: SH-01, SH-02, SH-04

RN-02 / SR-02 2. Decentralized Coordination & Comms

Need (RN-02): Researchers need to investigate decentralized architectures where robots exchange information directly and make autonomous decisions without a central server.
Req (SR-02): Onboard compute and peer-to-peer wireless communication supporting dynamic topologies.

Priority: Essential · Stakeholders: SH-01, SH-03

RN-03 / SR-03 3. Consistent & Predictable Robot Behavior

Need (RN-03): Researchers need consistent locomotion, sensing, and state estimation across units so outcomes reflect algorithmic performance rather than hardware variance.
Req (SR-03): Uniform motor response, odometry, localization, and sensor performance across the fleet.

Priority: Essential · Stakeholders: SH-01, SH-03, SH-05

RN-04 / SR-04 4. Adaptability to Different Research Problems

Need (RN-04): Researchers and developers need to evaluate diverse algorithms, sensors, and actuators across studies without redesigning the core robot.
Req (SR-04): Modular mechanical mounting and standardized electrical/software expansion interfaces.

Priority: Important · Stakeholders: SH-01, SH-02, SH-03

RN-05 / SR-05 5. Reliable Long-Term & Fleet-Level Operation

Need (RN-05): Operators and technicians need to run multiple robots concurrently over extended sessions with minimal downtime for charging or maintenance.
Req (SR-05): Integrated battery protection, rapid battery replacement, fleet charging, and modular serviceability.

Priority: Essential · Stakeholders: SH-01, SH-04, SH-05

RN-06 / SR-06 6. Environmental Awareness & Interaction

Need (RN-06): Researchers need robots to navigate shared environments safely, avoid collisions, and optionally transport or manipulate objects.
Req (SR-06): Onboard perception for collision avoidance (Important) and optional manipulation attachments (Desirable).

Priority: Important / Desirable · Stakeholders: SH-01, SH-02, SH-03

RN-07 / SR-07 7. Experimental Monitoring & Data Collection

Need (RN-07): Researchers and operators need to observe and record individual and fleet behavior without interfering with decentralized autonomy.
Req (SR-07): Non-intrusive centralized fleet monitoring, telemetry streaming, and synchronized logging.

Priority: Important · Stakeholders: SH-01, SH-03, SH-04

RN-08 / SR-08 8. Usability, Reproducibility & Tech Transfer

Need (RN-08): Collaborators and students from varied backgrounds need to onboard quickly, reproduce experiments, and adopt the platform across labs.
Req (SR-08): Intuitive interfaces, accessible diagnostics, and comprehensive documentation.

Priority: Desirable · Stakeholders: SH-01, SH-04, SH-05, SH-06
